Before we begin, a disclaimer: Expect a lot of googled info, guesswork, and completely made up stuff in regards to the music scene. Also, to avoid having to constantly research real world locations or worry about taking too many liberties with them, the game will take place in fictional cities without mentioning which states they are in, similar to Springfield in Simpsons and Brockton Bay in Worm.

Despite this adventure being about a pop group touring the globe, my knowledge of geography and locations around the world, even my own country, is pretty horrible. And even though I'm a big fan of both western and Asian music, the little bit of research that I've put into this setting so far has enlightened me on how little I know of what actually goes on behind the scenes in the music industry and band tours. I definitely got hit hard with the Dunning-Kruger effect as I got deeper into developing this campaign concept, to the point that I almost abandoned it before starting. But I figured that probably nobody would ever read this or care as much as I do about the discrepancies, so fuck it.

It makes me wonder whether this is another reason why everyone else plays adventures in fictional fantasy settings, since they can just make up how the world works without fear of anyone calling them on it. Urban fantasy is surprisingly difficult in contrast because everyone can fact check you.

Also, though Summer is the primary point-of-view character of this adventure, I created full character sheets for all three members because I expect to alternate between them. Each of the three women has their own personal struggle and nemesis, so it seems natural that there will be scenes where switching the perspective to a different member would make more sense.

Summer couldn't have been more relieved to see the sign for the Last Stop Music Hall appear through the windshield of their tour bus. It felt like she and her group had been driving for ages before they finally pulled into the parking of the venue where the group would be playing the following night. The booking of this event was somewhat short notice. The group had originally planned on playing in a city a few states over, and that event was still on track, but a little birdie told them that there had been potential demon sightings in this location. And so here they were, looking forward to giving a kick-ass show and kicking some demonic ass while they were at it.

The pop stars Summer, Miho, and Eunbi, along with their manager Marty, got off the bus and headed indoors.

The music hall was smaller than the venues that the group typically played for, but still a respectable size. It pleased Summer that there would likely be a good-sized crowd, despite the event being a last minute thing.

Marty approached the reception desk with confidence, but it became clear after conversing with the receptionist for a few moments that their show was in trouble. "What do you mean you double-booked?" he asked, displeased.

The poor receptionist, a young woman who looked to have graduated from high school only a couple years ago, was flustered and embarrassed by the situation. "I'm very sorry, sir. I'm not quite sure how this happened, but another act reserved the venue for tomorrow night, just as your group did."

Marty sighed in frustration, running his fingers though his short, styled hair. "Well, who was it?"

"My apologies, but it's against our policy to disclose that information."

His repeated attempts to persuade the receptionist to breach the privacy of her client eventually took its toll as her mood transitioned from ashamed to incensed.

With a groan, Marty turned to the three pop stars and said, "This is going to take some time. Why don't you ladies go explore the city while I deal with this?"

The women weren't in the mood for debating with management—and that was Marty's area of expertise, anyway—so they followed his suggestion.

"See ya, Marty," Summer said with a wave behind her as she walked away from the reception desk.

Miho bowed, saying, "Goodbye, Haynes-san," before spinning on her heels and jogging to catch up with Summer.

The three popstars hailed a cab to a nearby tourist attraction, Natoli Tower. The structure was absurdly tall and seemingly held no function beyond looking cool and generating tourism revenue. For the women, however, it was the location at which they'd agreed to meet their informant.

The informant in question was waiting for them on the topmost floor of the tower, because of course he was. Summer tried not to let the painfully long elevator ride to the top annoy her, but Eunbi had no reservations about making her annoyance known, as the Korean woman bitched the entire ride up. Miho kept quiet, as usual, seemingly unbothered by the lengthy elevator trip and her bandmate's pacing and bemoaning.

Summer was just grateful that they'd somehow managed to snag an elevator alone. Perhaps the crowd of tourists was lighter at this time of day? Each of the three women were sporting incognito chic anyway, decked out in baseball caps, dark sunglasses, and frumpy clothing to avoid drawing attention to themselves. It's difficult to combat the forces of hell while having to stop and sign autographs.

Summer wasn't entirely successful in avoiding notice by the crowd. There were elbow nudges and whispers among some of the tourists who clearly recognized the famed singer, but none approached her. Miho and Eunbi followed closely behind, safely avoiding exposing their identities, as if the fans couldn't put two and two together to guess who they were.

The women slipped into the free chairs at the table with their informant and tried not to call further attention to themselves. The man grimaced at them as he ended his phone call. He grumbled, "You girls aren't very good at this undercover stuff."

He wasn't wrong, but Summer still didn't appreciate the criticism. "I'd like to see how well you'd hide in plain sight while your face was plastered on billboards all over the city," she countered in a grump, to which her informant just grunted in response.

The gruff man sitting at the table, their informant, was Joel Merchant, a detective for the local police department. A few years back, the good detective had discovered a conspiracy of a biblical level involving demons possessing those at the top of the entertainment industry, perhaps with reach beyond even that. Knowing that he'd never be taken seriously by his peers and superiors in law enforcement, he'd reached out to the famous music group Death by Pop when he'd come across a seemingly ludicrous rumor that the three songstresses releasing singles topping the music charts were also slaying legions of the damned in their off-time. The women had only ever been in contact with Detective Merchant through email and phone before now, and Summer feared that she wasn't making the best first impression in person.

Attempting to save face, Summer brushed aside her failure at remaining incognito and asked the detective what information he had for them.
